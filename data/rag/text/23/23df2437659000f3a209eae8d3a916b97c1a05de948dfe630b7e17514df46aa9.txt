Keywords: Myxidium Parasites   of   fish SSU   rDNA Myxozoans ABSTRACT Coelozoic   parasites   of   the   family   Myxidiidae   were   observed   in   fish   of   the   order   Characiformes   captured   in   the middle   Tocantins   River,   Maranhão,   Brazil,   within   the   transition   between   the   Cerrado   savanna   biome   and   the eastern   extreme   of   the   Brazilian   Amazon   Forest.   The   analysis   of   the   morphological   characteristics   of   the   para¬ sites,   complemented   with   a   Bayesian   phylogenetic   analysis,   supported   the   description   of   new   specie   Myxidium imperatrizensis   n.   sp.,   found   parasitising   the   gallbladder   of   specimens   of   the   fish   Triportheus   angulatus.   The   study   is also   only   the   second   report   of   the   occurrence   of   Myxidium   parasites   in   fish   of   the   family   Triportheidae   in   Bra¬ zilian   rivers.   The   results   of   the   present   study   expand   the   known   distribution   of   the   genera   Myxidium   the   basin   of the   Tocantins   River,   the   largest   hydrographic   basin   located   entirely   within   Brazil,   which   encompasses   parts   of both   the   Cerrado   and   Amazon   biomes. 1.   Introduction Myxozoans   of   the   family   Myxidiidae   Thélohan,   1892   are   primarily coelozoic   parasites,   although   a   few   species   are   histozoic   [1].   Myxidiids infect   vertebrate   and   invertebrate   hosts,   in   both   aquatic   and   terrestrial environments   [2,3].   The   genus   Myxidium   Biitschli,   1882   is   the   most diverse   myxidiid   genus,   with   more   than   232   recognised   species   [1,4], followed   by   Zschokkella   Auerbach,   1909,   in   which   approximately   97 valid   species   have   been   described   to   date   [5].   Most   of   these   species   are parasites   of   marine   and   freshwater   fish   [6-8]   .   Phylogenetic   analysis   of this   group,   based   on   the   few   specimens   of   Zschokkella   that   present published   information   on   the   SSU   rDNA   gene,   revealed   four   distinct clades,   each   containing   members   of   this   genus   and   others   Myxidiidae, revealing   a   strong   relationship   with   the   hosts’   habitat   and   in   the   tropism of   tissues   [5.6]. The   most   recent   review   of   the   myxidiids   that   parasitise   New   World fish   reported   the   occurrence   of   33   Myxidium   species   and   five   species   of the   genus   Zschokkella.   However,   only   eight   Myxidium   species   have   been found   in   Brazilian   rivers   up   to   now   [9,10],   which   almost   certainly   rep¬ resents   a   very   minor   fraction   of   the   true   diversity   of   these   genera   in tropical   South   America   [2], With   a   large   variation   in   relation   to   the   shape   of   the   spore,   which varies   from   spindle-shaped   ellipsoidal   to   slightly   curved   to   sigmoids,   the main   morphological   character   used   in   the   differentiation   between   the genera   Myxidium   and   Zschokkella   is   the   shape   and   direction   of   opening   of the   polar   capsules.   While   Myxidum   has   polar   capsules   at   or   near   the   end of   the   spore   and   which   generally   open   in   opposite   directions,   in Zschokkella   the   polar   capsules   are,   in   most   cases,   almost   spherical   and slightly   subterminal   and   both   cling   to   one   side   of   the   sporal   body   [1,5]. Material   and   methods 2.1.   Sample   collection   and   morphological   analysis Fish   were   collected   on   the   Tocantins   River   between   September   2017 and   August   2019   using   hand-lines,   resulting   in   the   collection   of   70 Triponheus   angulatus   (Characiformes:   Triportheidae).   The   specimens were   collected   within   the   urban   zone   of   two   towns,   Imperatriz   (5.52°S, 47.49°W)   and   Estreito   (6.55°S,   47.45°W),   in   western   Maranhão   state, within   the   eastern   extreme   of   the   Amazon   Forest   biome.   Specimen collection   was   authorised   by   Brazilian   Biodiversity   Authorization   and Information   System   (SISBIO   44396-2). The   specimens   were   transported   to   the   Ecology   and   Limnology Laboratory   of   the   Maranhão   State   University   of   the   Tocantins   Region, where   they   were   anaesthetised   and   euthanised   for   parasitological   ex¬ amination.   The   experimental   procedures   were   approved   by   the   Ethics Committee   for   Experimental   Research   in   Animals   of   the   Federal   Rural University   of   Amazonia   (CEUA/UFRA   no.   013/2014). Once   the   presence   of   parasites   was   detected,   photomicrographs   of the   fresh   material   were   obtained   by   optical   microscopy,   and   by   Differ¬ ential   Interference   Contrast   (DIC)   to   determine   the   morphometric   pa¬ rameters   of   the   spores   of   the   parasites   found   in   the   gallbladder   of   the hosts.   The   morphometry   of   the   myxidiids   parasites   of   freshwater   Bra- zilianfish   was   compared   using   a   Principal   Components   Analysis   (PCA), run   in   PAST   3.0   [11,12]. Gallbladders   containing   spores   were   fixed   in   Davidson   solution   for 24   h   and   processed   for   histological   sectioning   [13].   For   Scanning   Elec¬ tron   Microscopy   (SEM),   the   contents   of   the   gallbladders   containing   cysts was   fixed   for   24   h   in   5%   glutaraldehyde   and   post-fixed   in   2%   osmium tetroxide,   dehydrated   in   an   increasing   ethanol   series,   dried   to   the   critical point,   and   metallised   [14]   to   obtain   images   using   a   TESCAN   Vega   3   LMU electron   microscope   in   the   Ultrastructure   Laboratory   of   the   Institute   of Animal   Health   and   Production   at   the   Federal   Rural   University   of   Ama¬ zonia   in   Belém,   Brazil. 2.2.   Extraction   and   sequencing   of   the   DNA DNA   samples   from   the   parasites   were   extracted   using   a   commercial PureLink   ®   Genomic   DNA   kit   (Invitrogen,   EUA),   following   the   manu¬ facturer's   specifications.   Segments   of   SSU   rDNA   were   amplified   by   PCR 2

The   PCR   was   run   in   a   final   reaction volume   of   25   pl   using   Master   Mix   Taq   polymerase   (PROMEGA,   Madison, USA),   with   the   following   parameters   adjusted   for   the   first   amplification cycle:   initial   denaturation   at   94   °C   for   5   min,   followed   by   36   cycles   at 94   °C   for   30   s,   60   °C   for   40   s,   and   72   °C   for   45   s,   with   a   final   extension   at 72   °C   for   5   min.   The   second   round   of   amplification   was   based   on   initial denaturation   at   94   °C   for   90   s,   followed   by   30   cycles   at   94   °C   for   20   s, polymerisation   at   46   °C   for   30   s,   and   extension   at   65   °C   for   60   s,   with   a final   extension   at   65   °C   for   10   min. The   PCR   products   were   visualised   by   electrophoresis   in   agarose   gel and   purified   using   an   ExoSAP-IT®   PCR   Product   Cleanup   kit   (GE   Life Sciences),   to   remove   the   excess   primers   and   dNTPs.   The   products   were then   sequenced   using   Big   DyeTerminator   v3.1   (Applied   Biosystems, Foster   City,   CA)   in   an   ABI   3100   sequencer   (Applied   Biosystems). 2.3.   Phylogenetic   analyses The   final   sequences   were   assembled   in   the   Codon   Code   Aligner software   (Codon   Code   Corporation,   Dedham,   Massachusetts)   and compared   with   data   obtained   from   GenBank,   using   the   Basic   Local Alignment   Search   Tool   (BLAST)   of   the   National   Center   for   Biotech¬ nology   Information   (NCBI).   The   multiple   alignment   of   the   SSU   rDNA sequences   was   run   in   BioEdit   [18],   with   the   ambiguous   regions   and variable   segments   with   no   phylogenetic   signal   being   excluded   [19,20]. The   substitution   models   for   the   Bayesian   Inference   (BI)   were   selected using   the   Bayesian   Information   Criterion   (BIC),   run   in   jModeltest   2.1.10 [1   9,20,22],   which   identified   the   GTR   +   G   model   as   the   most   appropriate nucleotide   substitution   model   for   the   SSU   rDNA   dataset.   The   Markov Chain   Monte   Carlo   (MCMC)   algorithm   was   executed   in   BEAST   v.1.8.4 [21,23]   with   10,000,000   generations   sampled   every   10,000   steps   [24], The   BI   result   was   used   to   reconstruct   the   phylogenetic   tree,   using   a database   that   included   40   sequences   of   different   species   from   the   fam¬ ilies   Chloromyxidae,   Myxidiidae,   Sphaeromyxidae,   and   Sphaerospor- idae.   Buddenbrockia   plumateliae   (AY074913)   and   Tetracapsuloides vermiformis   (KX013243)   were   included   as   an   external   group. 3. The   infections   were   identified   due   to   the presence   of   spores   floating   in   the   bile   fluid   and   immature   forms   asso¬ ciated   with   the   internal   epithelium   of   the   gallbladder. Discussion Despite   the   considerable   diversity   of   fish   found   in   the   tropical   zone   of South   America   [25,26],   few   data   are   available   on   the   myxidiid   parasites associated   with   these   fish   [2,4].   Although   Myxidiidae   is   the   second   most diverse   group   of   myxozoans   in   the   world   [1,10]. The   present   study   describes   one   new   specie   of   myxozoan   from   the Myxidiidae   family   that   parasitise   the   gallbladder   of   characiform   fish collected   on   the   middle   Tocantins   River,   in   the   transition   zone   between the   savanna   environment   of   northeastern   Brazil   and   the   eastern   end   of the   Brazilian   Amazon   Forest   biome.   This   is   only   the   second   report   of   a species   of   Myxidium   parasitising   a   triportheid   fish   in   Brazilian   conti¬ nental   waters   [1   0],   the   first   being   the   description   of   the   occurrence   of M.   cruzi   in   the   gallbladder   of   Triportheus   nematurus   in   the   Paraguay   River basin   [1,10].   The   description   of   Myxidium   imperatrizensis   n.   sp.   it   is   the first   report   of   the   occurrence   of   parasites   of   this   genus   in   fish   of   the species   Triportheus   angulatus,   and   it   also   represents   an   extension   of   the known   geographical   area   of   this   genus   of   myxozoans   to   the   Tocantins River   basin. The   analysis   of   the   morphological   characteristics   of   the   mixid   species described   here,   together   with   the   results   of   phylogenetic   analyzes, supported   the   distinction   of   both   Myxidium   imperatrizensis   n.   sp.   of   the other   mixidids   described.
